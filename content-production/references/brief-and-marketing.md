# Brief, message, and marketing outcome

Use this for campaign concepts, launch assets, social content and packaging, not as a compulsory form for a simple cut or image edit.

## Start with the smallest useful content bet

Identify what is actually known about the project and audience. Distinguish a working product, prototype, service offer and unvalidated idea. Pick the asset's job: explain a problem, demonstrate value, answer an objection, collect qualified interest, support onboarding, or make a concrete offer. Choose a useful audience action and success signal for that stage; views/likes alone do not establish sales or demand.

For an early venture, prefer a truthful demonstration or useful explanation with a coherent CTA before scaling volume or buying reach. Use a simple working visual direction when no brand exists: readable type, restrained palette, a consistent product depiction, and an ordinary human voice. Treat these as revisable assumptions, not a fabricated brand history.

A compact brief can fit in a paragraph: audience/problem, value/offer, proof available, main message, intended action, channel/format, tone, budget and deliverable. Reuse the user's current context. Ask only for a missing fact that changes the result; do not demand market research for a small draft. If choosing the business itself is the actual task, use product-management/brainstorming instead of manufacturing validation through content.

## Make the piece specific

Use a concrete opening that earns attention through relevance: show the task, result, problem, contrast supported by evidence, or a useful question. Show rather than narrating unproven claims. Product demos should connect the user's problem to a visible real action/result. Educational pieces can end with a useful takeaway rather than a forced sales pitch.

When useful, draft a few materially different hooks or treatments, each testing a clear hypothesis. Do not generate variants that merely swap adjectives. A/B assets should change a meaningful variable and use a comparable audience/distribution window; do not claim the winner from tiny noisy engagement or mock metrics.

Keep claims traceable to supplied facts or verified sources. A user asking for persuasive copy has not supplied evidence of savings, customer counts, revenue or testimonials. Preserve exact offers/prices/dates and flag missing substantiation. Use real customer quotations only with appropriate source/permission; synthetic UGC is not a real testimonial.

## Package and learn

Connect the script, visual evidence, on-screen text, thumbnail/title/caption and landing destination to the same promise. Adapt composition and pacing for each format rather than stretching one export. Suggested starting canvases include vertical 9:16 for short mobile video, landscape 16:9 for walkthroughs, or portrait feed visuals; verify current channel/ad specs when delivering to a particular platform.

For a launch package, include only requested/useful components: the core asset, cover, caption, accessible transcript/captions, clear CTA/link, and a bounded measurement idea. Examples of meaningful outcomes are qualified inquiries, completed onboarding or purchases; define what counts before evaluating. Do not create ad campaigns, audiences, trackers, scheduled posts, or spend without authorization. Record observed results separately from the initial hypothesis, including weak/no signal.
