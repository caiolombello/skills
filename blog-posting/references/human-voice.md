# Voice and evidence in articles

Use this for the article-specific side of voice. For recipient, register, personal samples, and cross-format editing, see [natural-writing](../../natural-writing/SKILL.md). For repeated stock phrasing, consult [ai-tells.md](ai-tells.md).

## Specificity without fabrication

A concrete mechanism is more useful than an adjective. A measured result can be stronger still, but only when supplied or verified. For example, these claims require different evidence:

- "The service starts faster": a comparison that needs support.
- "The service loads the SDK only when a request needs it": an implementation detail that can be verified.
- "Cold starts fell from 1.2s to 300ms": a measurement that needs actual results and test conditions.

Do not turn the first sentence into the third to make the prose vivid. Without measurements, describe what is known and qualify the effect. Not every paragraph needs a number, a name, or an anecdote.

## Stance follows the format

An opinion piece needs the author's actual position and a fair treatment of relevant objections. A tutorial needs working instructions. An explainer may remain neutral. Do not invent disagreement, confidence, or personal stakes to make the author sound more human.

A supplied experience report can use the author's first person. A research-based article should attribute its sources without pretending the author ran the experiment or lived the story. If experience is essential and missing, ask for the material or change the proposed format with the user's agreement.

## Editing an existing voice

Preserve distinctive wording, useful openings, humor, and the author's degree of certainty. A good existing draft may need very few changes. Do not force contractions, fragments, asides, or slang across the whole article.

Judge rhythm by reading and comprehension. Split a sentence that tangles two ideas; keep a long sentence that remains clear. Uniform lengths are not proof of AI authorship, and there is no required variance or minimum number of short sentences.

For newly written prose, prefer ordinary punctuation to sentence-connecting dashes. Keep grammatical hyphens, exact quotations, technical notation, and identifiers intact. Follow an explicit style request when it differs.

## Translation

Preserve the source's facts, opinion, uncertainty, and examples. Use idioms natural to the target language without carrying over awkward syntax. Do not localize currencies, replace tool names, change dates, or invent familiar local anecdotes unless adaptation is requested and the changes are grounded.

## Delivery

Use the requested surface: inline prose for a copy-ready post, or a file when asked. Keep needed attribution in the article. If missing author material would strengthen an otherwise complete draft, mention it separately only when useful; never insert invented details or unresolved placeholders into publishable copy.
